# Data Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary

This report details the analysis conducted on a dataset comprised of 1,000 entries, focusing on various media titles and their associated ratings. Key data quality issues were identified, including a significant portion of missing values and potential anomalies in the dataset's categorical components. Despite these issues, exploratory data analysis revealed several trends related to rating levels and release years. Recommendations are included to improve the dataset's quality and enhance future analyses.

## 2. Dataset Overview

The dataset consists of 1,000 rows and 7 columns, capturing information primarily related to media titles, their respective user ratings, and associated metadata such as release year and rating levels. Notable observations from initial data profiling include:

- High null percentage in the `user_rating_score` column (39.5%).
- High cardinality in the `title` column with 496 unique entries.
- Low cardinality in the `user_rating_size` column, indicating potential categorical nature.
- Limited distinct values in the `rating` column, suggesting a controlled list.

## 3. Data Quality

Quality assessments revealed several issues requiring attention:

- **User Rating Score**: High number of missing values with potentially significant impact on data insights.
- **Rating Level**: Moderate number of missing values (5.9%).
- **Rating Description**: Incorrect data type identified, affecting automated analysis processes.
- **User Rating Size**: Low cardinality suggests potential misinterpretation of numerical data as categorical.

Cleaning actions were performed to mitigate some of these issues; however, further steps should be considered for substantial improvement.

## 4. Key Findings

### Supported Claims

1. **Rating Levels and User Rating Scores**: 
   - A correlation of 0.1335 suggests a modestly positive relationship between higher rating levels and increased average user rating scores.

2. **Release Years and User Rating Scores**: 
   - A correlation of 0.1608 indicates that movies released more recently tend to receive higher user rating scores, suggesting a temporal trend in user preferences or rating criteria.

### Refuted Claims

Several hypotheses related to user rating size and its correlation with rating score variability, extremity, and general increases were tested and refuted:

1. No significant relationship found between user rating size and the extremity, variety, or increase of user rating scores.

2. More restrictive rating levels ('R', 'NC-17') do not necessarily correlate with different average user rating scores compared to less restrictive levels ('G', 'PG').

## 5. Visualizations

Four visualizations support the findings, offering a graphical representation of key insights:

- **Correlation Heatmap**: Overview of relationships among numeric columns.
- **Boxplots**:
  - Distribution of user rating scores across different rating levels.
  - Variations in user rating scores across different release years.

## 6. Recommended Next Steps

To enhance the dataset's utility and analysis depth, several actions are recommended:

1. **Imputation Strategies**: Implement robust approaches to address the high percentage of missing values in the `user_rating_score` column.
2. **Data Type Correction**: Re-evaluate and correct incorrect data types, such as those for `ratingdescription`.
3. **Categorical Analysis**: Explore further transformations for columns with low cardinality (`user_rating_size`) to ensure appropriate interpretation.
4. **Continuous Quality Monitoring**: Regular checks and updates to maintain data integrity and relevance.

In conclusion, while the dataset effectively highlights certain influential factors in media ratings, addressing identified quality issues will significantly enhance its potential for more accurate and actionable insights.
