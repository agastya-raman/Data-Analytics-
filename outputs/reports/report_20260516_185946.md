# Data Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary

The analysis of the dataset, which encompasses information on 891 passengers, highlights several significant findings regarding survival rates based on various attributes. Key insights include the increased survival of passengers in higher classes, females, those with family aboard, and those who paid higher fares. Additionally, individuals embarking from Cherbourg had higher survival rates. Data quality issues, particularly regarding missing values, were identified and addressed to enhance the accuracy of the analysis.

## 2. Dataset Overview

The dataset consists of 891 rows and 12 columns, primarily containing demographic and transactional details of passengers. Notable columns include 'PassengerId', 'Name', 'Ticket', 'Cabin', and 'Age'. The dataset was effectively profiled for potential issues, including null values and cardinality, which were promptly mitigated.

## 3. Data Quality

The dataset presents several quality issues that needed attention:

- **Missing Values:** 
  - 'Cabin' (77.1% missing values) needs careful handling due to high levels of missing data.
  - 'Age' (19.87% missing values) has a significant proportion of missing data, warranting imputation.
  - 'Embarked' (0.22% missing values) requires minor adjustments to manage null entries.

- **Cardinality Issues:** 
  - 'PassengerId' and 'Name' have unique identifiers for each entry and are not suitable for analysis.
  - 'Ticket' displays high cardinality, suggesting its use as an identifier instead of an analytical variable.

- **Potentially Incorrect Data Types:** 
  - 'Age', 'Fare', and 'Cabin' may benefit from parsing and binning for succinct analysis.

## 4. Key Findings

1. **Higher Class Survival Advantage:**  
   - Passengers in Pclass 1 had significantly higher survival rates than those in Pclass 3.

2. **Gender-Based Survival Rates:**
   - Female passengers had a higher survival rate compared to male passengers, with a substantial margin.

3. **Family Aboard:**  
   - Those traveling with family members exhibited different, often improved, survival rates compared to solo travelers.

4. **Fare Correlation with Survival:**  
   - A positive relationship was found between the fare paid and the likelihood of survival.

5. **Port of Embarkation:**
   - Passengers embarking from Cherbourg demonstrated higher survival rates than those from other ports.

6. **Age Influence on Survival:**  
   - Younger age groups experienced higher survival rates while the survival probability decreased with age.
