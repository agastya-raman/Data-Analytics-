# Data Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of a dataset containing 891 records and 12 variables. The analysis focuses on understanding key trends and patterns, particularly related to passenger survival rates and socioeconomic factors. Several data quality issues were identified, including missing values and outliers, that must be addressed to ensure the integrity of future analyses. Our exploratory data analysis confirmed some expected patterns linked to class and age while debunking others related to embarkation locations.

## 2. Dataset Overview

The dataset includes various features relevant to passenger demographics and journey details:

- **Total Records**: 891
- **Key Columns**: Age, Cabin, Embarked, PassengerId, Ticket, etc.
- **High Cardinality**: Ticket (681 unique values)
- **Unique Identifiers**: PassengerId and Name

The focus was on examining survival rates in relation to factors such as gender, age, class, fare, and family presence.

## 3. Data Quality

### Identified Issues

Several data quality issues were identified, necessitating intervention:

- **Age**: 19.87% missing data (177 entries), impacting analyses involving age.
- **Cabin**: 77.1% of data is missing (687 entries), requiring imputation or exclusion decisions.
- **Embarked**: Minor missing data (0.22%, 2 entries).
- **Fare**: Presence of significant outliers, potentially skewing financial analyses.
- **PassengerId**: Fully unique but offers limited analytical value beyond indexing.

### Severity

- **Age**: Medium severity due to substantial missingness.
- **Cabin**: High severity due to majority incompletion.
- **Embarked**: Low severity with limited missing entries.
- **Fare**: Medium severity attributable to outliers.
- **PassengerId**: Low severity related to uniqueness.

## 4. Key Findings

### Confirmed Insights

- **Gender and Survival**: Higher survival rates among female passengers, though the dataset indicates potential preprocessing issues with 'Sex'.
- **Class and Fare**: Passengers in higher classes paid significantly more on average (Pclass 1: \$84.15, Pclass 3: \$13.68).
- **Age and Survival**: Younger passengers have a slightly higher likelihood of survival, suggested by a weak negative correlation (\(-0.0772\)).
- **Family Influence**: Passengers with family members onboard exhibited a higher survival rate (50%) compared to solo travelers (30%).
- **Embarkation and Fare**: Passengers embarking from port 'C' paid higher average fares (\$59.95) compared to those from port 'S' (\$27.08).

### Refuted Hypotheses

- The hypothesis suggesting higher survival rates for passengers embarking from port 'C' compared to other ports was refuted.
- Single passengers were found to have comparable survival rates to those with family, refuting the initial hypothesis.
