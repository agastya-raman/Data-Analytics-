# Data Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of a dataset consisting of 891 entries and 12 attributes. Key findings indicate notable survival trends among different passenger groups on a ship, leveraging various data attributes including passenger demographics and travel details. The analysis reveals critical insights, particularly concerning survival rates across social classes, genders, age groups, and embarkation points. Despite some data quality issues, such as missing values in the `Age` and `Cabin` columns, the present findings maintain high confidence levels and offer actionable intelligence to guide future investigations and recommendations.

## 2. Dataset Overview

The dataset analyzed consists of 891 rows and 12 columns, encompassing critical information such as passenger demographics, ticket details, and survival status. Notably, the `PassengerId` and `Name` columns serve as unique identifiers for each passenger. Several columns, including `Age` and `Cabin`, have significant amounts of missing data, potentially impacting deeper analyses related to these attributes.

## 3. Data Quality

Data quality analysis surfaced four main issues:
- The `Age` column contains 19.87% missing values, which is a medium-severity concern given its potential analytical impact.
- The `Cabin` column, while highly diverse with 147 distinct values, suffers from a substantial 77.1% missing rate. This high missing data percentage poses a high-severity concern.
- A marginal 0.22% of missing data in the `Embarked` column indicates a low-severity issue.
- The `PassengerId` column has high cardinality, aligning with its role as a unique identifier, posing a low-severity issue related to uniqueness expectations.

## 4. Key Findings

1. **Class and Survival:**
   - Passengers from higher classes exhibited better survival rates, with first-class survival at 62.96%, second class at 47.28%, and third class at 24.24%. This finding confidently supports the socioeconomic influence on survival probability.

2. **Gender and Survival:**
   - Female passengers had markedly higher survival rates (74.2%) compared to males (18.89%). This trend highlights a significant gender disparity in survival.

3. **Age and Survival:**
   - Younger passengers showed slightly better survival prospects, as indicated by a correlation of -0.0772 between `Age` and `Survived`. Although the relationship is weak, it suggests age-related survival trends.

4. **Fare and Survival:**
   - There is a positive correlation (0.2573) between fare amount and survival rates, implying that passengers who paid higher fares had better chances of survival.

5. **Embarkation and Survival:**
   - Survival rates varied by embarkation point: 55.36% for port C, 38.96% for port Q, and 33.70% for port S, indicating the potential influence of embarkation locations on survival odds.
