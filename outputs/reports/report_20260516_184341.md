# Data Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary
This report provides an analysis of a dataset consisting of 891 rows and 12 columns, with specific focus on missing data and data quality issues. The analysis has uncovered important insights regarding the survival rates of passengers from the Titanic dataset, based on various factors such as gender, class, fare, family presence, and port of embarkation. These insights provide valuable information for understanding patterns in the data and can inform future analytical and operational decisions.

## 2. Dataset Overview
The dataset consists of records of Titanic passengers, with 12 columns capturing various attributes like **PassengerId**, **Survived**, **Pclass**, **Name**, **Sex**, **Age**, **SibSp**, **Parch**, **Ticket**, **Fare**, **Cabin**, and **Embarked**. It includes identifier fields, categorical variables, and continuous variables, catering to a wide range of analyses concerning passenger demographics, travel class, and survival outcomes.

## 3. Data Quality
Several data quality issues have been identified that could potentially influence the analysis:

- **Age**: Approximately 19.87% of the data in the **Age** column is missing. Given the importance of age in survival analysis, this is a significant issue requiring imputation or alternative strategies.
- **Cabin**: With 77.1% missing data, the **Cabin** column may not be reliable for analysis without substantial imputation or treatment.
- **Embarked**: A small percentage (0.22%) of missing data, which is minor but should be handled to ensure data completeness.
- **Fare**: Presence of outliers that might skew analyses requiring statistical testing or additional cleaning strategies.

These quality issues have been prioritized for remediation to ensure the integrity and reliability of the analysis results.

## 4. Key Findings
Through Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), the following key findings were confirmed:

1. **Gender-Based Survival**: Female passengers had a significantly higher survival rate (74.2%) compared to male passengers (18.89%), with high confidence supported by the data and tested claims.

2. **Class and Survival Rate**: Passengers in higher classes showed a markedly higher survival rate; specifically, Pclass 1 had a survival rate of 62.96%, while Pclass 3 had only 24.24%.

3. **Fare Influence**: There is a positive correlation (0.2573) between fare paid and survival, suggesting that passengers who paid higher fares had a higher chance of survival (medium confidence).

4. **Family Presence Impact**: Passengers traveling with family members had a different survival rate compared to those traveling alone, indicating family presence as a potential factor in survival chances (high confidence).

5. **Port of Embarkation**: Survival rates varied across different embarkation ports. Notably, passengers from port C had a higher survival rate of 55.36% compared to those from other ports (e.g., port S at 33.7%).
