# Data Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary
This report presents a detailed analysis of the dataset comprising information of 891 passengers from the Titanic. Through various analyses, we explored the correlation between passenger demographics and their survival rates. Key findings indicate higher survival rates among women, passengers in higher social classes, and those who paid higher fares. However, the dataset has crucial data quality issues, particularly in the `Cabin` and `Age` columns, which necessitate attention for comprehensive and accurate analysis.

## 2. Dataset Overview
The dataset contains 891 rows and 12 columns, including identifiers, demographics, ticket and cabin information, and survival status. Notably, some columns such as `Cabin` contain a high number of null values, significantly impacting potential analyses.

## 3. Data Quality
Data quality issues identified include:
- **Cabin Column**: Exhibits a high null percentage (77.1%) with 687 missing values. This could skew any related analyses unless addressed via imputation or exclusion.
- **Age Column**: Possesses 177 missing values (19.87%), which is problematic for analyses involving age demographics.
- **Embarked Column**: Contains only 2 missing values (0.22%), a relatively minor issue but still requiring rectification.
- **Fare Column**: Identified outliers that could distort statistical analyses and should be treated appropriately.

## 4. Key Findings
- **Gender and Survival Rates**: Women had a higher survival rate than men, a finding supported by high-confidence evidence.
- **Class and Survival Rates**: Passengers in higher classes (Pclass 1) showed significantly higher survival rates compared to those in lower classes (Pclass 3).
- **Fare and Survival Rates**: There is a positive relationship between the fare paid and the likelihood of survival, although this evidence holds medium confidence.
- **Age and Survival Rates**: A weak negative relationship suggests younger passengers might have slightly higher survival rates, but this finding is of low confidence.
- **Family Presence and Survival Rates**: Passengers with no siblings or spouses on board had different survival rates compared to those with at least one sibling or spouse.
- **Embarkation Port and Survival Rates**: Survival rates varied significantly depending on the port of embarkation, particularly favoring those from Cherbourg.
- **Family Size and Survival Rates**: Passengers traveling with family (higher combined SibSp and Parch values) experienced varied survival rates compared to those traveling alone.
