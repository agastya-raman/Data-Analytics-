# Data Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary
This report presents a comprehensive analysis of a dataset consisting of 891 entries across 12 columns, with a focus on exploring factors affecting passenger survival rates. The analysis supports several key hypotheses, including the impact of passenger class, gender, fare amount, and embarkation point on survival outcomes. However, the analysis refutes the hypothesis that younger passengers have higher survival rates than older ones. Several data quality issues have been identified, predominantly missing values in the `Age` and `Cabin` columns. Based on these insights, we provide recommended actions for data cleaning and further analysis.

## 2. Dataset Overview
The dataset comprises demographic and travel-related details of passengers. Key columns include `Survived` and `Pclass`, identifying whether a passenger survived and their class, respectively. The dataset also presents challenges such as high cardinality in the `PassengerId`, `Name`, and `Ticket` columns, and significant portions of missing data in the `Age` and `Cabin` columns.

## 3. Data Quality
The analysis identified several data quality issues:
- **High Severity**: Missing values in `Age` (19.87%) and `Cabin` (77.1%).
- **Low Severity**: Missing values in `Embarked` (2 instances).
- **Medium Severity**: Outliers in `Fare` and high cardinality in the `Ticket` column.
- **Low Severity**: High cardinality in the `PassengerId` column, mainly for unique identification purposes.

Three cleaning actions were implemented, addressing some of these issues to enable thorough exploratory data analysis (EDA).

## 4. Key Findings
Confirmed insights include:
- **Passenger Class**: Higher classes are associated with higher survival rates (1st class: 62.96%, 2nd class: 47.28%, and 3rd class: 24.24%).
- **Gender**: Females exhibit a significantly higher survival rate (74.2%) compared to males (18.89%).
- **Fare**: A positive correlation (0.2573) exists between fare amount and survival, indicating that passengers who paid higher fares improved their chances of survival.
- **Embarkation Point**: Survival rates vary by embarkation point, with the highest from `C` (55.36%).
- **Family Presence**: Passengers with family members had differing survival rates compared to those traveling alone.
- **Age Group**: Survival rates varied significantly across age groups.

Two claims were refuted:
- Younger passengers do not inherently have a higher survival rate compared to older ones.

## 6. Recommended Next Steps
To improve the dataset quality and build on current findings, we recommend:
- **Imputation of Missing Values**: Use statistical methods to impute the missing values in the `Age` and `Cabin` columns, and fill the missing entries in `Embarked`.
- **Feature Engineering**: Extract additional features from `Name` and `Ticket`, such as title and ticket prefix, for further analysis.
- **Outlier Analysis**: Conduct a deeper investigation into fare outliers to assess their impact on survival predictions.
- **Advanced Modeling**: Use machine learning models to further explore causal relationships and predict survival outcomes based on engineered features.

These steps will enhance data quality and yield more robust insights from the dataset.
